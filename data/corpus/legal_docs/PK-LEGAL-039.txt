JUDGMENT

Through this reference application the applicant assails the order of the
learned Appellate Tribunal Inland Revenue ("the Tribunal") whereby the
applicant's appeal against the order of the Commissioner Inland Revenue
(Appeals) was dismissed and the original assessment order, raising a
demand of approximately Rs. 1,750,000 on account of levy of regulatory duty, was upheld.

2. The principal questions of law proposed by the applicant for the
opinion of this Court are:

   (i) Whether the learned Tribunal was justified in upholding the order
       in the absence of any nexus between the impugned addition and the
       material on record?

   (ii) Whether the impugned order proceeds on a misreading of the
        provisions of SRO 845(I)/2018?

   (iii) Whether, on the facts and circumstances of the case, the
         applicant was afforded a reasonable opportunity of being heard?

3. Learned counsel for the applicant contends that the impugned order is
based on conjecture; that the books of account were maintained in the
ordinary course of business and were never rejected; that the additions
have been made on the strength of materials never put to the applicant;
and that the order is therefore violative of Section 122(9) of the Income
Tax Ordinance / corresponding provisions, as the case may be.

4. Learned Standing Counsel supports the impugned order and contends that
the applicant failed to discharge the burden cast upon him to explain the
discrepancies highlighted by the audit team.

5. Having heard the parties and perused the record, we find that the
foundational factual edifice of the impugned addition rests on a single
audit observation which was admittedly not formally put to the applicant
in the show-cause proceedings. The principle that no addition can sustain
in the absence of a fair opportunity is too well-settled to require
elaboration. See "Inspecting Additional Commissioner v. Pakistan Herald
Publications" 1995 PTD 1377 and "Edulji Dinshaw Ltd. v. ITO" 1990 PTD 155.

6. The questions of law are therefore answered in the NEGATIVE — i.e.
in favour of the applicant and against the revenue. The impugned order
is set aside. The matter is remanded to the assessing officer for a fresh
order after affording due opportunity to the applicant.

REFERENCE ANSWERED.
